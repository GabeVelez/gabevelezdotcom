The monster

Four states, and one of them is your window

Your sheet has four walk directions and four rush directions, which is not a guard's moveset — it is a boss with a commit. That is the whole design, and it is what makes a one-shot weapon fair.

STALK
rows 1–4
Walks straight at you, always. No patrol, no losing you, no cone to break — he knows where you are and he is coming. Slower than you, so you can open ground, and closing it is what builds the pressure.
speed 52
you move at 80
WIND UP
rush frame 1, held
Within reach he plants and rears back. This is the tell, and it is the only reason the fight is survivable — long enough to react, short enough to be frightening. He locks his direction here, to the nearest of four.
hold 400ms
at range 90px
RUSH
rows 5–8
Twice your speed, in a straight line, and it cannot steer. That single restriction is the fight: you do not outrun the rush, you step out of its lane. Ends on a wall or when the distance is spent.
speed 165
for 520ms
RECOVER
rush frame 5, held
Overshot, stopped, breathing. He cannot turn and cannot grab. In act two this is just when you put ground between you; in act three it is when you line up the shot.
hold 800ms
GRAB
Contact at any point, in any state. Same ending as being seen anywhere else in the game.
radius 20px
The full cycle is 1.7 seconds
400ms tell, 520ms rush, 800ms recovery. Roughly 26 rushes across the 45-second wait — enough that you learn the rhythm inside the first few, which is exactly what you want on a level people will replay.
No vision cone
The human villain downstairs has one and it is drawn on screen. This one should not: he is not searching, he is hunting, and giving him a cone would invite people to hide from something that cannot be hidden from.
Your inventory stops working
The box and the smoke grenade are dead from the moment he lands. Every trick that has carried you through nine levels stops here, and the level is what is left when they are gone.
Do it visibly, not silently. The two slots stay on screen and go dark as he hits the deck — a slot that quietly does nothing reads as a bug, a slot you watch die reads as the floor going out from under you. Then slot 3 lights up when the launcher drops. Taken away, then handed one thing back, and the one thing is not somewhere to hide.
