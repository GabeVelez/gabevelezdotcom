Half-a-Buck  /  Level 10

The Rooftop Finale

Three acts on one roof: you are hunted, the chopper arrives, you shoot your way onto it. The whole thing runs about ninety seconds.

Timings are from the moment you step out of the stairwell. Everything after the shot is cutscene, so the playable part is the first minute.

Before this — downstairs
The water lands, he goes down convulsing, and after a second the controls are taken away: the character breaks left round the desk and runs for the stairs on his own. Then the transformation plays. He is already gone when it happens, so he never sees it — only you do.
ACT ONE 0:00 – 0:12  ·  you are alone, and then you are not
0:00
Out of the stairwell
You arrive at the far left of the roof. The helipad is visible at the right-hand end, lit, empty. Nothing else is on the roof. The quiet is the point.
You know what is coming and he does not. Every second he spends walking calmly toward that helipad is worth more because of it.
0:10
You cross the trigger line
A third of the way along, heading for the helipad. Controls lock. A shadow appears on the deck ahead of you and starts growing.
0:11
He lands between you and the helipad
He appears over the landing spot at nearly three times his size and shrinks onto it, while a shadow grows beneath him. Impact, screen shake, dust ring. He holds a beat, then starts hunting.
Falling is drawn with scale, not position. Up-screen in a top-down view is away, not up — dropping him in from above the frame read as walking in from the north, and kept him off screen for most of it. The ground is the direction away from the camera, so falling toward it is something getting smaller: he starts big, which means he starts high, and he is in full view the whole way down.
Landing him between you and where you were going states the problem in one image. No dialogue needed, no explaining what changed.
On the same frame your box and smoke slots go dark. Nine levels of tricks, gone in the moment he arrives — a cardboard box is not going to help you here.
ACT TWO 0:12 – 0:57  ·  stay alive until the chopper gets here
0:12
The detection bar becomes a countdown
The HUD meter relabels to SURVIVE and fills over 45 seconds. Not "chopper inbound" — you have no way of knowing a helicopter is coming, and a bar that names it spoils its own arrival. The bar says what you have to do; the chopper is then the reward for having done it.
Agreed at forty-five. Long enough to feel like holding out; short enough that dying does not cost someone the whole level, and this is the last one.
0:12
↓
0:57
He hunts you across the roof
Stalk, wind up, rush, recover — the loop on the Monster board. He is slower than you walking and much faster than you rushing, and the rush cannot steer once it starts. You survive by reading the wind-up and stepping sideways.
A roof 768 wide and 180 deep is a big enough arena to keep circling and small enough that you can never fully break away.
ACT THREE 0:57 – end  ·  the way out arrives armed
0:57
The helicopter drops onto the pad
It comes down on the helipad at the right-hand end, rotor still turning. It does not leave. The monster keeps hunting throughout — the arrival changes nothing about the danger, only about your options.
1:00
A bazooka comes out of the door, and a shout
It lands on the deck beside the pad. One line over the rotor noise: “Not with that thing on the roof!”
This one line does the heavy lifting. Without it the obvious move is to walk past the weapon and get in, and the whole finale collapses. With it, the chopper is the goal and the reason you have to fight.
—
You pick it up and press it
It goes into slot 3, the same rotating slot the keycard and the glass of water used. Press it anywhere near him and the game takes over.
cut
Shot → hit → explosion → cigar
Four beats, no more play. The last one is you in the cabin, side on, cigar in your mouth, city going past behind. Then the ending screen.
